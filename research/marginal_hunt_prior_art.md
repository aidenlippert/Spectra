# Prior-art attack map: the learned boundary of physical 2-RDM marginals

Scope: primary-source audit for a proposed separation oracle / adaptive certificate generator for the convex set of fermionic \(N\)-representable 2-RDMs.

## The nearest direct hit: semidefinite machine learning (2026)

Delgado-Granados and Mazziotti, “Direct Variational Calculation of Two-Electron Reduced Density Matrices via Semidefinite Machine Learning,” arXiv:2603.05524 (2026), [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05524).

This obtains fully \(N\)-representable 2-RDMs from CASCI ground-state calculations, treats those exposed points as data-driven boundary points, and uses their convex-hull geometry to train a fully input-convex neural network (ICNN) barrier. The barrier is added to an SDP constrained by approximate conditions, notably DQG. Tests use three isoelectronic diatomics in a cc-pVDZ \([N_e=10,N_o=8]\) active space.

The overlap is direct: both learn boundary information inside semidefinite variational optimization. The proposed extension is a **direction-conditioned dual separation oracle**: given \(H,\Gamma\), propose \(\langle A,\Gamma'\rangle\ge b\) and verify it by an independent fermionic SOS identity. The paper’s ICNN is a learned convex penalty/classifier, not a list of certified facets. The exact set is not a polytope; finite exposed data points only approximate it. The reported split includes all data from other molecules and even bond geometries from the test molecule, so it does not establish transfer to wholly unseen molecules, orbital bases, periodic materials, or adversarial candidates. The paper reports one \(0.9\) Å misclassification; a penalty value alone is not a sound lower-bound certificate.

## Hastings: adjacent self-consistent SOS discovery

Matthew B. Hastings, “Improving Perturbation Theory with the Sum-of-squares: Third Order,” [arXiv:2412.03564v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03564v1). It gives a self-consistent perturbative ansatz for finding dual SOS decompositions, improving speed and accuracy on model Hamiltonians. The author states that the implemented ansatz is unsuitable for quantum chemistry with large diagonal terms, while suggesting a modified ansatz.

Matthew B. Hastings, “Improving Perturbation Theory with the Sum-of-squares II: Large Density-Density Terms,” [arXiv:2606.31765v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31765v1). It moves selected large density-density interactions into \(H_0\) and handles related density-dependent hopping and spin-spin terms, but remains a degree-6 SOS fragment and does not give singlet hopping the same nonperturbative treatment. These methods suggest proposal families for witness search; they do not supply a general \(N\)-representability separator.

## Dual-cone 2-RDM: the language of supporting hyperplanes

Mazziotti, “Dual-Cone Variational Calculation of the 2-Electron Reduced Density Matrix,” arXiv:2103.17155 (2021), [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.17155).

This formulates variational 2-RDM optimization over the polar/dual cone generated by \(N\)-representability conditions. It shows that the dual calculation gives ground-state energies at reduced cost for higher conditions such as \(T_2\), and recovers the 2-RDM via a Hellmann–Feynman/Lagrange-multiplier argument. Applications include hydrogen chains and FeMoco.

This supplies the compiler’s exact interface: the Hamiltonian selects a dual direction, and optimization searches a tractable subset of valid supporting operators. The missing mechanism is **adaptive discovery of the small set of dual generators that closes the energy gap for a new Hamiltonian**. Existing dual-cone work uses prescribed representability families and low-rank parametrizations; it does not learn a generally valid, Hamiltonian-conditioned SOS certificate language.

## What \(T_1/T_2\), 2-positivity, and subsystem constraints accomplish

The standard constraints require positivity of expectations of operator squares.
Cubic anticommutator constructions cancel six-operator terms and produce
T-type constraints on the 2-RDM, as described in the quantum Hall paper below.

The quantum Hall bootstrap application, [PRX Quantum Hall paper](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/csnn-vjhn) (2025, 15, 031034), imports these quantum-chemistry positivity constraints and discusses higher-\(p\) moment matrices. Higher constraints require structured tensor decompositions or larger moment objects. Every valid \(O^\dagger O\) identity is a dual-cone witness. The limitation is incompleteness: \(T_1/T_2\) describe a relaxation, and a PSD pseudo-marginal can still fail global \(N\)-representability. Local or subsystem constraints improve scaling, but overlap consistency does not automatically produce a global fermionic state.

## Complexity is a design constraint

Liu, Christandl, and Verstraete, “Quantum Computational Complexity of the N-Representability Problem,” [arXiv:quant-ph/0609125](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0609125), establish QMA-completeness of \(N\)-representability. This rules out treating unrestricted exact membership as a routine polynomial-time primitive absent major complexity consequences. It leaves a promise class open: chemically relevant Hamiltonian directions may admit short certificates even when arbitrary membership is hard.

## The unexplored leverage

Learn **certificates, not facets**. Parameterize a witness

\[
W_\theta=\sum_k B_k(\theta)^\dagger B_k(\theta)+\text{fermionic identity terms},
\]

with all terms beyond two-body order eliminated symbolically or retained in an explicitly consistent lifted relaxation. Train \(\theta\) against the current primal SDP residual and Hamiltonian direction. A symbolic checker verifies the SOS identity and particle-number/symmetry constraints; only checked witnesses enter the dual cone:

\[
\text{candidate }\Gamma\rightarrow\text{learned witness proposal}\rightarrow\text{exact SOS verification}\rightarrow\text{new cut}.
\]

Benchmark the number and rank/degree of verified cuts needed to close the primal-dual gap on stretched bonds, transition-metal active spaces, periodic fragments, and adversarial Hamiltonian directions. Withhold chemical families and orbital rotations to measure transfer; independently check every generated inequality to measure soundness.

## Verdict

The field already has convex 2-RDM optimization, dual supporting-operator formulations, ML barrier approximation, and perturbative/self-consistent SOS witness construction. The concrete target is their combination into a Hamiltonian-conditioned **sound adaptive separation oracle** whose proposals are certified by compact fermionic SOS identities. This note makes no priority claim and does not infer novelty from an incomplete literature search.
