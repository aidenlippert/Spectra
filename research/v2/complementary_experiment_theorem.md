# Exact complementary transfer in a thermal two-qubit experiment

This replaces the rejected interacting-Hamiltonian draft. It is a finite statistical theorem about experiment selection, not a claim of universal mechanism discovery.

## Model and access

The unseen target is a two-qubit thermal state

`rho_{s,a,b} = (I + s v P_a ⊗ P_b)/4`,

where `s ∈ {−1,+1}`, `a,b ∈ {0,1}`, `P_0=X`, `P_1=Z`, and known `0<v≤1`. The three hidden bits are independent and uniform. The laboratory may make one of the four local product-Pauli measurements `M_{ij}=P_i⊗P_j`. Its plus outcome is Bernoulli with

`Pr(+ | s,a,b,i,j) = (1+s v 1[i=a]1[j=b])/2`.

Thus a mismatched measurement is a fair coin and carries no information about `s`; a matched measurement is a noisy sign observation with bias `v`.

The response axes are generated by public binary knobs but unknown fixed masks: `a(x)=m_A·x mod 2` and `b(z)=m_B·z mod 2`, with `m_A,m_B` uniform. Calibration sources expose computational-basis observations through a BSC with crossover `eta`. With noiseless calibration, two independent contexts identify each two-bit mask exactly; with noise, majority vote on repeated contexts recovers each mask with the usual Hoeffding confidence bound. The target uses an unseen `(x,z)` combination and receives no hidden-map metadata.

## Exact one-shot theorem

Consider the four measurement choices as the complete one-shot menu. Let `R` be Bayes error for recovering `s`.

* Neither map transferred: any selected `(i,j)` matches the hidden `(a,b)` with probability `1/4`. Conditional on a match, the optimal sign decision has error `(1-v)/2`; on a mismatch it has error `1/2`. Therefore `R_0=1/2-v/8`.
* Only `A` transferred: choose `i=a(x)` and any `j`. Matching probability is `1/2`, hence `R_A=1/2-v/4`.
* Only `B` transferred is symmetric: `R_B=1/2-v/4`.
* Both maps transferred: choose `i=a(x),j=b(z)`, always matching, so `R_{AB}=1/2-v/2`.

The complementarity score is

`J = R_A + R_B - R_0 - R_{AB} = v/8 > 0`.

This is an exact mixed discrete derivative for the one-shot Bayes risk. The proof is exhaustive over the four-measurement menu: conditional on each hidden axis pair, a mismatched product measurement has identical Bernoulli law for `s=±1`, so no decision rule can improve on error `1/2`; on a matched pair, the likelihood-ratio rule attains `(1-v)/2`. Averaging gives the formulas and proves optimality for this menu.

## Acquisition and noisy calibration

Calibration is part of the cost. The learner receives `d` independent public contexts per side, with binary observations of the corresponding linear map through BSC(`eta`). For `d=2` linearly independent contexts and `eta=0`, the mask is exactly identified. For `eta<1/2`, repeat each context `r` times and use majority; Hoeffding bounds each inferred bit error by `exp[-2r(1/2-eta)^2]`. A union bound over the four mask bits gives confidence at least `1-δ` when `r ≥ log(4/δ)/(2(1/2-eta)^2)`. On calibration failure, target risk is bounded by adding its failure probability; no perfect-transfer claim is made under noisy calibration.

### Dependency-respecting acquisition of B

Suppose only the direct A-calibration source is available. After learning `a(x)`, learn `b(z)` from a known-positive reference state `rho_{+,a(x),b(z)}` at fixed nonzero `x`. Set `i=a(x)` and `j=0`. Conditional on A being correct, the plus probability is `(1+v)/2` when `b(z)=0`, and `1/2` when `b(z)=1`. The following randomized postprocessing produces a binary B-label: on a plus outcome output `1` with probability `r=v/(2+v)`; on a minus outcome output `1` always. Direct calculation gives

`p_0=[1-v+r(1+v)]/2=1/(2+v)`,

`p_1=(1+r)/2=(1+v)/(2+v)=1-p_0`.

Thus the postprocessed label is exactly a BSC with crossover `eta_B=1/(2+v)`; for `v=1/2`, `eta_B=2/5`. Majority over independent compiled-reference shots therefore learns the B mask with the standard Hoeffding bound using bias `v/(2+v)`; the total failure probability obeys `δ_total ≤ δ_A+δ_B` (the B guarantee is conditional on A correctness). Physical reference draws and all shots are counted as acquisition cost.

This postprocessing is the current certified learning API. A direct likelihood-ratio threshold on the raw plus/minus counts can improve the constant and should be a future baseline; no superiority claim is made here.

For `N` independent targets, transferred mechanisms cost their acquisition once and reduce target Bayes risk from `R_0` to `R_{AB}`. An equally informed Bayesian optimal-design agent may match the same choices; this theorem does not establish algorithmic superiority.

When a transferred map is unavailable, choose the unknown axis uniformly at random. Averaging over that randomized action makes the stated `R_0`, `R_A`, and `R_B` formulas hold for every fixed hidden mask, so conditioning on a calibration-success event does not introduce mask-selection bias. If both inferred maps fail with probability at most `δ`, the realized AB risk is at most the ideal risk plus `(v/2)δ`; consequently the ideal complementarity score degrades by at most `(v/2)δ` (a looser `4δ` additive bound is also valid).

As a restricted cost comparison, with `v=1/2` and calibration failure at most `0.01`, the stipulated four-shot target-only comparator has risk `103/256≈0.4023`, whereas one transferred AB measurement has risk `1/4+0.01=0.26`. Thus the comparator needs at least five target shots to beat the one-shot transferred protocol. If acquisition consumes `C` shots, amortization against this four-shot baseline begins once `N>C/4`; this accounting is only for the stated menu and protocol, not an unrestricted lower bound on adaptive target-only inference.

## Scope and loopholes closed

The state family is a valid positive two-qubit density operator for `0<v≤1`; no incompatible-Hamiltonian dynamics or claimed sign-sensitive evolution is used. The result concerns one-shot inference with a fixed measurement menu. Adaptive multi-shot policies, mask-learning design, and arbitrary POVMs are outside this theorem and may change the risks. There is no claim that the hidden maps are discovered in an unrestricted sense: they are finite GF(2) response laws inferred from calibration observations and transferred to an unseen basis combination.
