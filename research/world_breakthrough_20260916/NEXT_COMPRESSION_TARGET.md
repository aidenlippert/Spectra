# Field-level breakthrough target: constructive correlated-matter compression

Date: 2026-09-16

- The strongest consequential target is a **constructive, composable compression theorem for strongly correlated fermions**: from local Hamiltonian data and a checkable correlation parameter, produce a compact retained/environment response together with efficiently verifiable upper and lower error bounds. The parameter could be a certified gap/decay/Markov quantity, but it must be computed or bounded from the input rather than inferred after a full solution.

- The proposed distinction is simultaneous control of representation size, certified error, and composition through repeated elimination **for difficult interacting fermionic classes beyond the settings already covered by efficient theorems**. None of compact states, lower bounds, or renormalization alone is new. A small tensor ansatz with an exactly evaluated variational energy supplies an upper bound, and existing methods already provide rigorous results in restricted settings. The research target is a useful nonperturbative response certificate whose construction does not hide a full Hilbert-space solve; this note does not establish that no such conjunction exists in any special class.

- The half-filled repulsive Hubbard reflection-positive construction is an exact special case, but it does not provide this theorem. Its one-spin operator still contains all balanced-sector amplitudes in a dense representation. The particular repulsive half-filled reduction does not automatically extend to doping, frustrated hopping, or general molecular integrals; Lieb's separate attractive-model theorem has a different filling scope. Primary source: [Lieb, Phys. Rev. Lett. 62, 1201 (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.62.1201).

- The mathematical deliverable should therefore be a theorem with an explicit failure mode: under stated locality/gap/decay hypotheses, the response enclosure has size and error bounded by a declared function of the correlation parameter and boundary size; outside the hypotheses, the method returns an invalidity diagnosis or a weaker certified bound. A post-hoc low-rank fit, scalar residual, or sampled PSD test is not enough to establish this.

- The general-Hamiltonian caveat is fundamental. Fixed-basis electronic structure is QMA-complete in the worst case: [O'Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman, arXiv:2103.08215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08215). This is not an unconditional impossibility theorem. A uniform polynomial-time classical solver for all such inputs would imply a major complexity-class collapse; an efficient method for an identified physical class need not. An unsupported promise of universal efficiency is therefore inappropriate. Failure to certify the structural hypotheses need not itself have a short proof; the algorithm may report that its bound is inconclusive.

- The decisive physics validation should be a **doped two-dimensional Hubbard benchmark at the stripe/superconducting competition**, with a finite-size sequence and a thermodynamic extrapolation. Freeze the response-construction rule before the test; certify energies and selected pair/charge observables; compare against sign-problem-controlled AFQMC where available, DMRG/iPEPS finite-size references, and cold-atom measurements. The target is a resolved energy/observable difference and an uncertainty interval that distinguishes competing stripe and pairing regimes, not merely another larger lattice. Relevant primary benchmarks include [Qin et al., Phys. Rev. X 10, 031016 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.031016) and [Xu et al., Science 384, eadh7691 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh7691).

- A real-chemistry validation should be a secondary transfer test: a held-out family of transition-metal spin-state gaps or bond-breaking energy differences, with solver error separated from basis, geometry, environment, and relativistic model error. Agreement with NIST data alone is retrospective validation; the stronger result is a frozen prediction whose interval changes an experimental candidate choice.

- Scope: this note is a mathematical distinction and prioritization, not a claim that the theorem exists or that Spectra has achieved it. The first publishable breakthrough could be a restricted theorem with a useful correlation parameter and a fully coupled 2D or molecular demonstration; it need not solve every many-body Hamiltonian. The world-level claim becomes credible only after independent replay, complete discovery accounting, transfer beyond half filling, and comparison to the strongest methods on equal resources.

Current comparison as of 2026-09-16: [Gu et al., Nature Communications 17, 7838 (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74028-6) report variational neural-state Hubbard calculations up to 16 by 16 sites, including stripe structure, and use variance as an error proxy when exact energies are unavailable. [Wang and Devereaux (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16616) report finite-temperature pairing signatures, and [Zhang and von Delft (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25022) report finite-temperature simulations down to approximately 0.004t. A new method must be assessed against these substantive existing results. Merely reaching a larger site count or observing a pairing signal does not define the proposed breakthrough.
