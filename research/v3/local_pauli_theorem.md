# A constructive compact approximation after an interacting coupling

## What this adds

The original exact-closure experiment grew rapidly after a ZZ interaction. This construction shows a restricted way around that obstruction: retain an approximate local observable model, admit strong known local depolarization, and certify the error directly in physical expectations. The representation can stay small as the chain grows.

This is a deterministic mathematical approximation procedure with supplied Hamiltonian and noise parameters. It does not show that a learner discovered the procedure, establish an advantage over matched stateful methods, or close the compounding-science goal. Related local simulation principles already follow from [quasilocality for Markovian quantum dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4210); no novelty is claimed.

## Admitted dynamics

Let `H=sum_Z h_Z P_Z` contain only one- and two-site Pauli terms, with rational coefficients, and define the incident interaction strength

\[
\kappa=\max_i\sum_{Z:i\in\operatorname{supp}(P_Z)}|h_Z|.
\]

The Heisenberg generator is `L(P)=i[H,P]-gamma w(P)P`, where `w` is Pauli weight. This specifies independent local depolarization using the convention that each nonidentity local Pauli decays at rate `gamma`. The target observable is one single-site Pauli. Time is in the corresponding Hamiltonian units with hbar set to one.

For the coefficient representation `O=sum_P a_P P`,

\[
|\operatorname{Tr}\rho O|\le\|O\|_\infty\le\sum_P|a_P|=\|a\|_1
\]

for every density operator. There is no Hilbert-space-dimension factor in this inequality.

## Uniform contraction

The absolute coefficient sum in the commutator column of a word with weight w is at most `2 kappa w`: only Hamiltonian terms touching its support can anticommute, each contributes at most `2|h_Z|`, and summing incident strengths may overcount but is safe.

The logarithmic matrix norm induced by coefficient l1 is therefore at most

\[
\mu_1(L)\le\max_{P\ne I}[-\gamma w(P)+2\kappa w(P)]
\le-(\gamma-2\kappa)=-\lambda
\]

when `gamma>2kappa`. Identity is excluded and cannot be generated by a commutator of Pauli observables. Thus `||exp(Lt)||_1 <= exp(-lambda t)`. Deleting rows and columns cannot increase these column sums, so every principal retained or discarded block obeys the same bound.

This is a sufficient strong-damping condition, not a universal threshold. Local depolarization erases information; it does not protect quantum memory.

## Short-time paths and a finite representation

Generate a retained set S containing the seed and every Pauli word reachable by at most R nonzero Hamiltonian commutators. The reduced generator is the principal projection `L_S`. No diagonalization of the full operator space is needed to construct S: only frontier commutators are evaluated.

Expand the exact propagator in a Dyson series around the diagonal damping generator. Diagonal damping changes no Pauli support. Starting from weight one, after j Hamiltonian factors the weight is at most `j+1`, since every two-site term that acts nontrivially must touch the existing support. Consequently the sum of absolute path weights after k factors is bounded by

\[
\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}2\kappa(j+1)=(2\kappa)^k k!.
\]

The ordered integration simplex has volume `t^k/k!`; damping factors have norm at most one. The k-th term is bounded by `(2kappa t)^k`.

Every path of length at most R is entirely inside S. The reduced Dyson expansion consists exactly of full-system paths that remain in S, with identical diagonal factors and coefficients. Subtracting it leaves only paths that exit S. Therefore there is no extra factor of two in the short-time tail:

\[
\|e^{Lt}a_0-\iota e^{L_St}a_0\|_1
\le\frac{(2\kappa t)^{R+1}}{1-2\kappa t},
\qquad 2\kappa t<1.
\]

Here `iota` embeds the reduced coefficient vector. The argument requires the complete depth-R neighborhood; selecting only apparently important paths does not inherit this certificate automatically.

## All-time certificate

Choose a rational cutoff tau with `x=2 kappa tau<1`. On `[0,tau]`, monotonicity bounds the short-time tail by `x^(R+1)/(1-x)`. For `t>=tau`, contraction of both propagators gives the bound `2 exp(-lambda tau)`. Hence

\[
\sup_{t\ge0}\sup_\rho
\left|\operatorname{Tr}\rho\,[O(t)-\widehat O(t)]\right|
\le
\max\left\{\frac{x^{R+1}}{1-x},\,2e^{-\lambda\tau}\right\}.
\]

This is an absolute observable-error theorem for all states under the admitted generator. It is not a global-state reconstruction theorem. The implementation encloses the exponential by rational positive-series and geometric-tail arithmetic, and a separate checker verifies the model, neighborhood coverage, norm bound and threshold.

## Constructive prediction and numerical error

For the small projected coefficient generator A, choose `q=max_P gamma w(P)` over retained words. Then `M=I+A/q` has induced l1 norm at most one. Uniformization gives

\[
e^{At}=e^{-qt}\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{(qt)^m}{m!}M^m.
\]

All retained vectors and weights are rational for rational t. The code bounds the omitted positive scalar tail geometrically and encloses `exp(-qt)` from the same positive series. Multiplying the scalar interval width by the exact retained-vector l1 norm, then adding the Poisson-tail bound, gives an explicit numerical error. This adds to the physical truncation certificate.

The implemented predictor has declared exponent and series-term caps and refuses computations outside them. The all-time structural theorem does not mean every requested numerical prediction has zero cost. Very late times can also be approximated by zero with the direct contraction bound, though that shortcut is not needed for the recorded predictions.

## Measured finite construction

The tested chains use nearest-neighbor XX strength 1, nearest-neighbor ZZ strength 1/5, and on-site Z strength 1/2. Their maximal incident strength is `kappa=29/10`. Take `gamma=300`, `tau=1/32`, and request absolute error at most `1/1000`.

Depth R=4 yields a uniform bound approximately **0.00023891244**. Retained sizes are 30, 98, 109, 109 and 109 for 3, 6, 12, 24 and 48 qubits. The equal sizes after boundary effects disappear arise from the fixed local neighborhood; they are not an empirical claim about arbitrary interactions.

The 48-qubit full nonidentity Pauli space has `4^48-1` coordinates. That number is a comparison of ambient spaces, not a benchmark of a strong alternative algorithm: local simulation methods need not build that full space either.

Strong damping does not make every omitted interaction harmless at the requested accuracy. At t=1/1000, prepare X-positive neighbors and a Y-positive central spin, leaving other sites maximally mixed. A damping-only model predicts zero for the central Z observable. The reduced prediction and its full physical/numerical certificate instead imply expectation greater than **0.00232**, exceeding the allowed error 0.001. Thus this particular trivial baseline fails even in the strong-damping regime. No conclusion about optimal conventional methods follows.

## Costs and limits

Construction scans the supplied Hamiltonian terms and the finite commutator frontier. Verification repeats those bounded local operations; it never forms a `4^N` matrix. Prediction cost is polynomial in the retained matrix/vector size, series length and rational bit precision, all represented explicitly. A full Pauli numerical calculation is used only on three qubits as an independent cross-check.

The Hamiltonian, depolarization law and damping lower bound are supplied. Learning or experimentally verifying them remains a separate cost and inference problem. Unknown interactions outside the model can invalidate the guarantee. General multi-site interactions require a different support-growth bound. Weak damping or excessive representation size causes the present method to decline certification.

The result repairs one precise many-body bottleneck under a favorable physical restriction. Turning this certificate into something an investigator discovers and reuses with a measured learning advantage remains the active research obligation.
