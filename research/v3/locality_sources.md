# Locality and dissipative simulation sources

These results support the project’s finite-support/local-Pauli certificates as standard locality consequences. They do not establish a universal many-body solver, a compact causal abstraction for arbitrary interventions, or a self-correcting memory.

## Primary sources

1. **Barthel and Kliesch, “Quasilocality and Efficient Simulation of Markovian Quantum Dynamics,” PRL 108, 230504 (2012).** [APS primary record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.230504), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4210). For time-dependent Markovian master equations with short-range interactions, their generalized Lieb–Robinson bound shows that Heisenberg evolution of an observable can be approximated by dynamics restricted to a neighborhood of its initial support, with an error decreasing exponentially in the neighborhood diameter (for fixed time and locality parameters). They derive system-size-independent classical local simulation costs and efficient quantum Trotter simulation under their stated assumptions. The theorem concerns local observables and finite times; it does not make global state evolution or arbitrary long-time steady-state computation efficient.

2. **Kliesch, Gogolin, and Eisert, “Lieb-Robinson Bounds and the Simulation of Time Evolution of Local Observables in Lattice Systems,” arXiv:1306.0716 / book chapter (2014).** [arXiv primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0716). This gives a unified statement for local Liouvillians and Hamiltonian lattice systems: bounded-range/decaying local generators produce a finite propagation velocity and an approximate causal cone for observables. The review explicitly separates locality of an observable from complexity of reconstructing a global state and discusses simulation consequences and finite-size effects. Use it for definitions and hypotheses, not as an additional universal-computation theorem.

3. **Cubitt, Lucia, Michalakis, and Pérez-García, “Stability of Local Quantum Dissipative Systems,” CMP 337, 1275–1317 (2015).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.4744), [primary record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/9rh1g-br410). For local Lindbladians with sufficiently decaying interactions, a unique fixed point, and mixing time scaling logarithmically with system size, local observables and correlations are stable under polynomially decaying perturbations. The hypotheses are substantive: uniqueness and rapid mixing are assumed, and the result is stability of local behavior, not preservation of encoded quantum information. It therefore cannot be used to infer self-correction or a protected memory lifetime.

## Relation to the project certificate

The existing Pauli/Dyson calculation is a finite-dimensional, explicitly checkable special case: truncate the generator to a retained operator support and bound the omitted commutator terms. Lieb–Robinson/quasilocality results justify why such a support truncation can have controlled error for local observables at bounded time when the physical generator satisfies the relevant locality and norm assumptions. They do not validate a claimed residual bound outside that declared time, interaction, or noise domain.

For a local depolarizing term with rate `gamma`, a condition such as `gamma > 2 max_x sum_{X contains x} ||h_X||` can be a useful sufficient dominance condition in a particular local norm estimate: dissipative contraction can dominate the Hamiltonian’s local commutator growth. It is not a theorem of self-correcting memory. Strong local depolarization destroys local information and generally drives the system toward a simple fixed point; without a separately encoded, recoverable sector and a proven lifetime scaling, it is active erasure, not passive protection. The threshold is model- and norm-dependent and must not be reported as universal.

## Bounded theorem worth proving here

For a finite-range Lindbladian on a finite lattice, a declared retained Pauli set, bounded local term norms, and time horizon `T`, prove an independently checkable observable error bound combining (i) the exact retained-generator residual and (ii) a Lieb–Robinson tail for terms outside radius `R`. Report dependence on `T`, `R`, local dimension, interaction norm, and dissipative rates. Include an abstention result when the bound exceeds tolerance. This would strengthen the existing certificate while making no unsupported claim about global simulation, universal synthesis, or quantum memory.
