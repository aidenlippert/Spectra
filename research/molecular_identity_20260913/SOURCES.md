# Primary-source audit: local CAR witnesses and adaptive constraints

## Established N-representability antecedents

The matrices generated by local cubic CAR operators are valid CAR positivity maps: for any cubic operator `A` on any chosen orbital labels and any particle-number sector, `A†A >= 0` identically. A generic cubic block probes degree-six moments (the 3-RDM level). It should therefore not be identified directly with `T1` or `T2`: those are particular three-index sums/anticommutator constructions whose degree-six terms cancel or reduce, leaving constraints expressible through the 2-RDM. `T2'` strengthens `T2` by adding the standard one-body/number-derived correction used in practical SDP calculations.

Primary computational evidence is **Nakata et al., “Variational calculation of second-order reduced density matrices by strong N-representability conditions and an accurate semidefinite programming solver,” JCP 128, 164113 (2008)**, [full paper](https://t2r2.star.titech.ac.jp/rrws/file/CTT100570254/ATD100000413/jcp08%2Bsecured.pdf). It explicitly compares `P,Q,G,T1,T2'`, states that `T2'` implies `T2`, and reports sub-millihartree effects from replacing `T2` with `T2'` in molecular/Hubbard calculations. **Mazziotti, “Significant Conditions on the Two-electron Reduced Density Matrix from the Constructive Solution of N-representability,” 2012**, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0541), derives the broader `(2,q)`-positivity hierarchy, including three-index conditions and polynomial-scaling subsets.

Consequently, relabeling a cubic `A` onto new orbitals needs no special lifting theorem for soundness: the CAR identity `A†A >= 0` remains true for every particle number. The unproved questions are whether the resulting degree-six moments are available in the chosen relaxation, whether the added blocks improve the bound, and how their number and solve cost scale with system size. If the implementation uses a local fixed-N identity or sector-specific contraction, that extra assumption needs separate justification. A witness found in one optimizer iterate is a useful discovery signal, but its numerical coefficient vector does not by itself prove tightness or useful H6 scaling.

## Subsystem and cluster restrictions

**Nakata, “On the size-consistency of the reduced-density-matrix method and the unitary invariant diagonal N-representability conditions,” 2011**, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.5665), proves size consistency under stated assumptions: unitary-invariant diagonal N-representability, each subsystem 2-RDM corresponding to a fixed electron-number eigenstate, and at least one selected `P,Q,G,T1,T2'` condition. This is a direct warning for local-to-global claims: subsystem number-sector assumptions are part of the theorem, and a locally PSD block alone does not establish compatibility of assembled marginals.

**Li and Lu, “Quantum variational embedding for ground-state energy problems: sum of squares and cluster selection,” 2023**, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18571), gives a primary example of selecting clusters to tighten a quantum SOS/RDM lower-bound relaxation under a computational budget. Cluster selection is therefore established adaptive relaxation design. A new claim would need to show that the H4 negative directions form a reusable, low-description-length selector or cutting-plane oracle rather than merely adding whichever violated SDP constraints are discovered.

## Mathematical pitfalls for the proposed H4→H6 transfer

1. Positivity of `A†A` is an operator identity, but a negative eigenvalue of a finite moment/dual block indicates that the current moment assignment violates that valid constraint (or that the projection/normalization is inconsistent). A generic cubic block requires degree-six moments; the reduced `T1/T2` forms are special constructions, not generic cubic blocks.
2. Three-index positivity conditions are necessary, not generally sufficient, for full N-representability. The exact N-representability problem is computationally hard: **Liu, Christandl, and Verstraete, “N-representability is QMA-complete,” 2007**, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0609125). Therefore a few added cubic blocks cannot be described as a complete certificate without a separate completeness theorem.
3. A direction `y` extracted from an H4 creator-channel witness can be transferred by relabeling the operator, because CAR preserves `A†A >= 0`. What must be tested separately is whether the H6 relaxation contains the required degree-six moments and whether the transferred block materially tightens the bound.
4. Adaptive cutting-plane generation is valid only if every added inequality is globally valid, even when discovered from an approximate primal/dual solution. Numerical violation is a discovery signal, not a proof of invalidity or of a new exact constraint.

The defensible novelty boundary is thus: “generate and retain valid local cubic positivity constraints” is an ordinary consequence of CAR/RDM positivity. A potentially novel contribution would instead concern a demonstrably effective reusable selection/compression rule, with measured tightness and scaling; soundness of the relabeled operator itself follows immediately from CAR and is not the hard theorem.
