# Collective remainder and retained interference

The active question is whether a small retained interference structure can
coexist with a collectively certified remainder and controlled coupling. A
smaller SDP with the same large coefficient map does not meet that objective.

This pass constructs a restricted, explicitly parameterized model family:
four interacting active fermion modes, two extensive bath bands, two active–bath
coupling patterns, and collective charging interactions. In a fixed total-N
sector, charging interactions reduce exactly to an active-number polynomial.
Particle/hole bath energy envelopes preserve the exact filled-bath energy.
Each envelope has degenerate bins; only one bright mode per bin couples to the
active system, while dark spectator occupations are minimized analytically.

Acceptance requires exact rational PSD checks of every feasible retained
particle sector and a rational upper witness for the upper operator envelope.
Every collective substitution, number constraint, metric factor, and band
endpoint is reconstructed. Numerical eigenvalues propose witnesses only.

Tests will grow bath multiplicity at fixed active/pattern count, perturb bath
dispersion, compare one versus two bins, and compare independently assembled
small full Hamiltonians with both envelopes. The largest retained system is
eight modes; the large physical Fock sector must never be constructed by the
certificate paths. A molecular scope audit will measure the residual and rank
obligations of this representation on the existing H4/H6 fixtures; a successful
structured control must not be reported as molecular transfer.

The mathematical ideas have antecedents in impurity/bath reduction, spectral
operator order, and collective charge symmetry. The repository already has
implicit Dicke–Schur certificates. No first-in-literature or universal
many-body compression claim is part of this pass.
