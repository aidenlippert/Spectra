# Targets 5 and 8: constructive control and physical synthesis

## Promise-domain formulation

For target 5, an instance should contain a finite-dimensional (or explicitly truncated) Hilbert space, computable descriptions of `H0` and control Hamiltonians `Ha`, a bounded control class (amplitude, bandwidth, switching count), initial state, target state or observable, time budget `T`, noise channel, and tolerance `epsilon`. The theorem must return either a pulse `u` with an independently checkable bound

`D(E_u(rho0), rho*) <= epsilon`

or a sound obstruction for the same budget: a conserved quantity, separating observable, speed-limit bound, or other certificate proving the target is outside the robust reachable set. Exact arbitrary-state preparation over unrestricted dimensions is not this problem.

Finite-dimensional Lie-algebra criteria establish existence-level controllability under their model assumptions. The dynamical Lie algebra generated by `iH0, iHa` being `su(N)` (with standard variants) supports operator controllability, but does not provide short pulses, noise robustness, or polynomial cost in a succinct many-body description. Schirmer, Pullen, and Solomon survey these criteria and distinguish dissipative cases: [Controllability of Quantum Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302121). This is an established theorem under stated assumptions, not a constructive universal matter-control result.

The promise restriction is necessary. Li and Ying prove several reachability properties for quantum automata undecidable in general: [Undecidable Problems about Reachability of Quantum Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6249). Fijalkow et al. prove undecidability for some finite-union control sets in linear systems and hardness connections to Skolem and Positivity problems: [Reachability in Linear Time-Invariant Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06575). These results block unrestricted #5/#8 claims; they do not block bounded robust subclasses.

For target 8, let `phi` be a trace/observable requirement, `Theta` a computable uncertainty class, and `B` a resource budget. Synthesis returns a fabrication/control policy `pi` and a proof that every admissible `theta` satisfies `Pr_theta,pi[phi] >= 1-delta`, or a sound impossibility certificate. Completeness should be claimed only on a separated promise domain: feasible instances have margin at least `gamma`, infeasible instances violate the requirement by at least `gamma`; boundary cases may remain unresolved.

## What delta-decision does and does not supply

Gao, Avigad, and Clarke’s original delta-decision theorem for bounded formulas returns one of two overlapping outcomes: the formula is unsatisfiable, or its delta-weakening is satisfiable. In the overlap region either answer is permitted; this is not an ordinary true/false decision and should not be described as generic “true, strengthened-false, or unknown.” See [Delta-Decidability over the Reals](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.6671). Their related bounded SMT result covers computable functions, including suitable ODE descriptions, with complexity bounds under assumptions: [Delta-Complete Decision Procedures](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3513). Kong, Solar-Lezama, and Gao extend the approach to bounded exists-forall formulas using interval propagation, counterexample-guided synthesis, and optimization: [Exists-Forall Delta-Decision](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08137).

These results justify tolerance-aware bounded synthesis. They do not imply a universal physical compiler: unbounded trajectories, arbitrary switching, discontinuities, noncomputable model descriptions, and exact boundary instances remain outside the guarantee. “Finite time” alone is insufficient. A grid approximation is complete only after proving a modulus: every admissible continuous control has a grid control whose induced observable trajectory differs by at most `eta`, with `eta` bounded from Lipschitz constants, control variation, integration error, and noise. The target must have a margin exceeding this discretization error.

## First actionable lemma

For a finite-dimensional open quantum system with Lipschitz dynamics, piecewise-constant controls on a rational grid, bounded duration `T`, and a target separated by `gamma`, prove a certificate-producing robust reachability lemma. Interval propagation must output either (i) a grid pulse with independently checkable error at most `epsilon`, or (ii) a dual lower-bound witness proving every pulse in the explored cell has error at least `epsilon+gamma`. The proof must expose dependence on dimension, Lipschitz constants, grid width, `T`, integration precision, and noise. This is a bounded bridge between #4’s certified dynamics, #5’s control, and #8’s refinement logic.

## Concrete experiment and composition failure

Use 4–8 qubits with local drift and controls. Compare (A) a family whose Lie closure is `su(2^n)` with (B) a symmetry-preserving family carrying a conserved charge. For random targets, require either a verified pulse or a verified charge-sector obstruction. Hold out a resonant coupling and test transfer. Compare against an optimizer that reports empirical fidelity only; record certificate validity, cost, and unresolved margin cases.

Composition must be tested explicitly. For two isolated spins, take `H_A=H_B=0`, initial state `|00>`, and local promises that `<Z_1>` and `<Z_2>` remain fixed; each reduced model is exact. The composed system adds the omitted interface coupling `H_int=g X_1 X_2`. Its exact evolution gives `<Z_1>(t)=cos(2gt)` (and likewise for spin 2), so the local residual can reach `2`, despite each isolated certificate being exact. This is a counterexample to composing abstractions that omit an interaction, not a violation of the isolated assumptions. If `g X_1X_2` is included in the joint generator and its norm/error is bounded, the refinement bound can compose. Interface variables, shared baths, memory, and conserved quantities therefore belong in the composition contract.
