# Finite-time residual certificate for Heisenberg observables

This is a conservative, finite-dimensional certificate.  Let `G` be the exact
Heisenberg generator on a chosen real Hermitian coordinate basis and let `P(s)`
be a piecewise polynomial approximation on `[0,T]`, with `P(0)` equal (or
certifiably close) to the target observable `O`.  Put

```text
r(s) = P'(s) - G P(s).
```

For a unital completely positive Heisenberg semigroup, `||exp(sG)||_{∞→∞} <= 1`.
Variation of constants therefore gives the directly checkable bound

```text
||O(T)-P(T)||∞ <= ||O-P(0)||∞ + Σ_j ||P(t_j+)-P(t_j-)||∞
                  + ∫_0^T ||r(s)||∞ ds.                    (1)
```

The same statement holds at every intermediate time by replacing `T` with
that time.  Jumps are included because a piecewise polynomial need not be
continuous.  No lower bound on a damping rate, spectral gap, or contractivity
of `G` itself is used; only dual unital-CP contractivity is used.  For a
non-unital Heisenberg map, supply an independently verified growth factor or
do not claim (1).

## Exact rational checker

Represent every coordinate of `P` and every matrix entry of `G` as `Fraction`.
On segment `[a,b]`, use the local variable `u=s-a` and store
`P_i(u)=Σ_{k=0}^d p[i,k]u^k`.  Form exactly

```text
r_i(u) = Σ_k (k+1)p[i,k+1]u^k - Σ_j G[i,j]P_j(u).
```

A numerical proposer may export rational polynomial coefficients. That exact
polynomial is the approximation being certified: recomputing its residual
already charges its difference from the exact evolution. No separate error
relative to an unrounded numerical algorithm is needed. If instead the claim
concerns uncertain polynomial coefficients or uncertain generator coefficients,
those uncertainties require separate enclosure terms; this checker currently
accepts a fixed exact rational generator and polynomial only.

For a Pauli coordinate basis, bound a Hermitian residual written as Pauli
words.  Partition terms into verified pairwise-anticommuting groups.  A group
`Σ_i c_i Q_i` has exact norm `sqrt(Σ_i c_i²)`; for leftover terms use the
triangle bound `Σ_i |c_i|`.  The implementation in `experiments/certificates.py`
already performs exact rational grouping and conservative rational square-root
bracketing.  Thus, at each `u`,

```text
||r(u)||∞ <= Σ_groups sqrt(Σ_i c_i(u)^2) + Σ_leftover |c_i(u)|.   (2)
```

Do not evaluate (2) only at endpoints: a polynomial can peak in the interior,
and cancellation between coordinates cannot lower a safe upper bound.

## Integrating the bound without numerical assumptions

The always-valid cheap bound is coefficient-wise Taylor triangle.  For a scalar
polynomial `q(u)=Σ_k q_k u^k`, on `0<=u<=h`,

```text
sup |q(u)| <= Σ_k |q_k|h^k,
∫_0^h |q(u)|du <= Σ_k |q_k|h^(k+1)/(k+1).                 (3)
```

Apply (3) to each leftover coordinate and to each anticommuting group after the
Euclidean inequality `sqrt(Σ_i c_i(u)^2) <= Σ_i |c_i(u)|`; this is weaker than
the pointwise group norm but remains exact rational and cheap.  A tighter exact
upper bound for a group uses coefficient vectors `v_k=(c_{i,k})_i` and
`||Σ_k v_k u^k||₂ <= Σ_k ||v_k||₂ u^k`, with rational upper brackets for each
square root, then integrates term by term.  This preserves the group gain
without needing root finding.

Optional tightening: convert each scalar/group component to the Bernstein basis
on `[0,h]`.  Since `u^k` is nonnegative in normalized coordinates and Bernstein
polynomials form a convex partition of unity, `sup|q|` is bounded by the max
absolute Bernstein coefficient; integrating the absolute envelope gives `h`
times the sum of absolute coefficients divided by `d+1` (or use subdivision for
tighter intervals).  Bernstein conversion is exact rational.  Use it only as a
tightening pass; Taylor (3) is the fallback and reference implementation.

For each segment, accumulate the resulting rational upper bound `I_m`; add
`Σ_m I_m`, the exact initial mismatch bound, and exact jump bounds.  Compare the
final rational/radical enclosure against tolerance (for example `1/1000`) only
after rounding outward.  A reported pass must include segment widths, degree,
term/group counts, initial and jump contributions, residual integral, and the
certificate bound.

## Coordinate choices and cost caps

The implemented checker accepts explicit sparse Pauli expansions only. It does
not accept arbitrary coordinate bases with an asserted conversion constant.
All omitted residual terms generated by the declared Hamiltonian must be
recomputed. Budget exhaustion is a refusal, never silent truncation.

The implementation caps system width, Hamiltonian terms, sparse support,
polynomial degree, number of pieces, total coefficients and rational bit length.
It binds the sum of segment durations to an externally requested horizon.
The counters are structural operation counts, not a complete bit-complexity
model. Complete timed evaluations must include construction, solving, witness
construction and independent checking, with failures and retries charged.

This is a numerical certificate procedure, not a new theorem: validate it on
small systems by comparing with dense Lindblad propagation, including the
requested `t∈[0.1,1]` and `gamma∈{0,0.2,2}` cases.
