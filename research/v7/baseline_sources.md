# v7 baseline sources (bounded primary-source receipt)

Scope: literature baselines for residual bounds on Lindblad observables, adaptive certificates, anticommuting-Pauli norm estimates, data-informativity/identifiability, and algorithm search. These are baseline procedures and assumptions, not novelty claims. URLs are primary papers or first-party records; accessed 2026-09-10.

## 1. Lindblad truncation and a posteriori certification

**Etienney, Robin & Rouchon, “A posteriori error estimates for the Lindblad master equation,” Quantum 10, 2031 (2026), DOI [10.22331/q-2026-03-16-2031](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2026-03-16-2031), arXiv [2501.09607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09607).** This is the directly relevant March-2026 revision/publication. It splits error into (i) finite-Hilbert-space truncation and (ii) time discretization, gives explicitly computable upper bounds, and uses them to adapt both time step and Hilbert-space cutoff. The guarantee is for the stated infinite-dimensional Lindblad model and the chosen finite truncation/integrator; it is not a generic many-body observable certificate. Cost is reduced by avoiding an unnecessarily large Fock cutoff, but the method still requires computable tail/coupling quantities and repeated finite-dimensional solves. This is the strongest baseline for a residual-driven adaptive certificate.

This paper is specifically about infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces (notably oscillator/Fock-style truncations) and its adaptive cutoff controls omitted basis states. That differs from v7’s finite-spin observable residual adaptation: a spin system already has a finite Hilbert space, so the proposed retained operator/Krylov space and boundary residual require a separate derivation. The useful transfer is procedural only: choose a retained subspace, compute the finite generator and a computable boundary/leakage term, integrate the residual bound over the time window, and enlarge the subspace until the observable certificate is below tolerance. Any transfer must state the norm and observable duality explicitly; positivity or trace preservation alone does not make a truncation bound available.

**Besse et al., “Adaptive low-rank approximation and denoised Monte-Carlo approach for high-dimensional Lindblad equations,” Phys. Rev. A 92, 062126 (2015), [arXiv:1509.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07960).** Provides an adaptive deterministic low-rank density-operator approximation with an on-the-fly estimator for reduction error, plus a Monte Carlo method. It is a practical adaptive baseline, but rank adaptation is not the same as a rigorous full-space truncation certificate; assumptions and estimator reliability are model/discretization dependent. Relevant cost scale is reduced rank rather than full density-matrix dimension.

**Gravina & Savona, “Adaptive variational low-rank dynamics for open quantum systems,” Phys. Rev. Research 6, 023072 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.023072).** Time-dependent variational integration dynamically changes a low-rank basis for Lindblad dynamics. It targets low-entropy states and reports time/memory savings, but is an approximation strategy rather than an a posteriori rigorous residual certificate. Keep it as a performance comparator, not as the proof baseline.

## 2. Krylov/Arnoldi residual baselines

**Jawecki, Auzinger & Koch, “Computable upper error bounds for Krylov approximations to matrix exponentials and associated phi-functions,” [arXiv:1809.03369](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03369).** Derives a rigorous a posteriori upper bound from the defect/residual of the Krylov exponential approximation, computable economically in the Krylov space, and gives step-size selection. Assumes a standard Krylov approximation to a matrix exponential (and extensions to phi-functions); it is not specifically Lindblad or observable-only. Baseline procedure: build Arnoldi basis, evaluate reduced exponential and endpoint residual, accumulate the bound over steps, and stop/enlarge when below tolerance. Main cost is matrix-vector products plus a small projected exponential; no full matrix exponential is required.

**Jia & Lv, “A Posteriori Error Estimates of Krylov Subspace Approximations to Matrix Functions,” [arXiv:1307.7219](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7219).** Gives two reliable a posteriori estimates for Arnoldi approximation to `exp(-tau A)v`, including a residual-like estimate and a first-term error-expansion estimate; also treats smooth matrix functions. Assumptions concern the Arnoldi/Krylov construction and matrix-function regularity. Use as a second independent residual baseline, with the caveat that “reliable estimate” and a rigorous upper bound depend on the theorem/variant selected.

For v7, a conceptual certificate can use the projected Lindbladian `H_m` and Arnoldi defect without adding a dependency: observable error is bounded by the dual norm of the state residual integrated over time, then multiplied by the observable norm. This supports larger certificates without constructing the full basis. Independent dense matrix propagation is limited to small systems (implemented checks use two and three qubits); larger cases use the exact sparse checker.

## 3. Anticommuting Pauli norm machinery

The primary algebraic source is the Clifford/Pauli representation: **Aaronson & Gottesman, “Improved Simulation of Stabilizer Circuits,” Phys. Rev. A 70, 052328 (2004), [arXiv:quant-ph/0406196](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406196)**, which formalizes Pauli commutation through binary symplectic products and Clifford updates. For a set of Hermitian Pauli words that pairwise anticommute, direct Clifford algebra gives `(sum_i a_i P_i)^2 = (sum_i a_i^2) I`; therefore the operator norm is exactly `sqrt(sum_i a_i^2)`. This identity is preferable to a triangle bound, but applies only after verifying pairwise anticommutation and Hermitian phase conventions. A native NumPy/Fraction implementation can verify the symplectic commutation bit and accumulate the squared coefficients exactly. For certified thresholds, enclose rational `s=sum_i a_i^2` by integers `p,q` satisfying `p² ≤ s q² < (p+1)²`, yielding `p/q ≤ sqrt(s) < (p+1)/q`; do not use an unspecified conservative float.

## 4. Informativity versus identifiability

**van Waarde, Eising, Trentelman & Camlibel, “Data informativity: a new perspective on data-driven analysis and control,” [arXiv:1908.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00468), revised v3 (2020).** The authors distinguish data informative for a target analysis/control property from data sufficient for unique system identification; some data-driven tasks are possible without unique identification, while others require persistently exciting data. For v7, phrase a certificate as “informative for this observable/residual bound under the tested generator class,” and separately report whether the full Lindbladian is identifiable. A successful adaptive query schedule does not establish that all unseen directions are determined.

## 5. Algorithm-search precedent

**Novikov et al., “AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery,” [arXiv:2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131).** Describes evolutionary search over executable algorithm proposals using an evaluator, with automated mutation/selection and examples in mathematics and systems. The transferable baseline is an outer search loop over certificate heuristics with a deterministic score: certified residual, cost, and failure penalties. It does not supply a physics-specific guarantee; every candidate still needs the explicit certificate and small-system oracle checks. A reproducible v7 version should keep the search optional and bounded, with exact `n=3–6` validation and no claim that search discovers a theorem.

## Recommended baseline protocol

For each adaptive truncation, compute (a) the projected evolution, (b) a boundary/residual quantity, (c) an integrated state-norm bound, and (d) the observable dual-norm conversion. Enlarge the retained space when the bound fails. Use independent dense propagation for selected small systems; at larger sizes report exact sparse certificates and complete computation costs without a hidden full-matrix calculation. Do not strengthen damping merely to force positivity: positivity is a model property, while the certificate must remain valid at the stated parameters.
