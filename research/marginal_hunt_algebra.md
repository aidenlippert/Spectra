# Hunting sparse two-body certificates in the fermionic marginal cone

## The algebraic lever

Let `CAR(M)` be generated by `a_p,a_p†`, and fix the `N`-particle sector. A
two-body Hamiltonian has an expectation value that is linear in the 2-RDM. A
certificate is therefore a normally ordered operator identity

`C - b I = sum_alpha B_alpha† B_alpha + Z`, with `P_N Z P_N=0`

whose left side has degree at most four. The number-sector term vanishes on all
admissible states. Positivity of the right side proves
`Tr(C Gamma) >= b` for every physical 2-RDM. The useful question is how a
degree-three `B` can contribute to a two-body (four-fermion) left side.

## Exact T1/T2 cancellation (the seed family)

For an antisymmetric coefficient tensor `t_{ijk}`, define

`A_3(t) = sum_{ijk} t_{ijk} a_i a_j a_k`.

The anticommutator `{A_3(t), A_3(t)†}` is positive in expectation and, after
CAR normal ordering, its six-operator (three-body) terms cancel exactly. What
remains is a linear combination of the three-particle metric matrices usually
called `^3D + ^3Q`, but represented entirely by 0-, 1-, and 2-body operators.
Requiring the resulting matrix to be PSD is the T1 condition.

For coefficients antisymmetric in the two creation indices, define the mixed cubic operator

`E_3(t) = sum_{ijk} t_{ijk} a_i† a_j† a_k`.

The same CAR calculation gives `{E_3(t), E_3(t)†}` with its degree-six part
cancelled, leaving the `^3E + ^3F` T2 matrix as a PSD constraint on the 2-RDM.
The strengthened T2' family adds a linear (one-body) piece to `E_3`, optimized
or fixed by a contraction relation; its square introduces cross terms that
remain two-body and can cut points that T2/DQG miss. These are exact identities,
not approximate reconstructions. The cancellation mechanism is explicitly
described in Mazziotti's hierarchy and in the modern quantum-Hall exposition
linked below.

In compact form, for either allowed cubic `B(t)`,

`< {B(t),B(t)†} > = < Gamma, M_t > + c_t`,

where `M_t` holds the surviving two-body coefficients. **This coefficient
matrix need not be PSD.** Positivity comes from the displayed sum of operator
squares; a PSD Gram matrix, when used, acts on the operator dictionary rather
than on the two-body Hamiltonian coefficients.

Verification is exact CAR rewriting and equality of all coefficients. A single
cubic square generally has degree-six terms. Its anticommutator cancels them:
the leading symbols are odd exterior polynomials and anticommute. This applies
to pure and mixed cubics, including a homogeneous-charge linear addition.
The executable diagnostic checks both cases independently against Fock action.

## The adaptive search space

Search sparse cubic coefficient vectors inside charge-homogeneous blocks and
select those with negative anticommutator expectation at the candidate 2-RDM.
This is selective T-type separation. No further degree-six cancellation equations
are needed for an anticommutator of odd polynomials of degree at most three.
For a general unpaired sum of cubic squares, cancellation remains a separate
linear coefficient constraint on the Gram matrix.

Mixing charge -3 and -1 blocks does not by itself strengthen fixed-number
constraints: cross-charge expectations vanish. Low Gram rank also does not
imply sparse coefficient vectors. Neither mixing these blocks nor using ML
establishes a new positivity family beyond existing T-type conditions.

The more ambitious direction is to learn homogeneous-charge dressed cubic-plus-
linear dictionaries and solve Hamiltonian coefficient matching with a PSD Gram
matrix. Expand the full identity, including all higher-order coefficients and
sector constraints, before accepting any proposed lower bound.

## Why this can cut beyond DQG

DQG tests positivity only for quadratic fermionic operators, so its PSD blocks
encode pair, hole-pair, and particle-hole metrics. T1/T2 come from cubic
operators and therefore impose coexistence constraints involving three
fermions, after the three-body component has cancelled. They are not implied by
DQG in general; numerically, 3-positivity is known to improve stretched-bond
and strong-correlation energies, at increased cost. T2' and recursively
generated linear constraints are especially relevant when full T2 is too large.

The sharp representation is thus not “all of the cone” but the cone generated
by verified low-rank CAR anticommutator certificates, searched adaptively in
the Hamiltonian's active orbital geometry.

## Evidence and limits

Established: T1/T2 are necessary 2-RDM conditions arising from nonnegativity of
cubic operators; the (2,q)-positivity hierarchy generalizes this and can be
expressed as direct 2-RDM constraints. Established: dual-cone 2-RDM methods use
these constraints to obtain lower energy bounds. Conjectural: a small,
Hamiltonian-conditioned rank-r subset will close useful chemical duality gaps;
there is no general efficiency theorem, and unrestricted N-representability
remains hard.
