# A restricted compression theorem for fermionic SOS certificates

## Purpose

The useful target is not a claim that every molecular SOS certificate is small.
The target is a sufficient, checkable condition under which a certificate is
small *and* its residual can be bounded without enumerating the full fixed-
particle-number Hilbert space.  The condition below is deliberately stronger
than chemistry in general.  Its value is that every assumption is exposed and
the certificate-size claim is a theorem rather than a slogan.

## Setting

Let `A` be a finite fermionic operator algebra on `M` modes, with CAR relations,
and let `P_N` project onto the fixed `N`-particle sector.  Let `J` be an
explicitly supplied operator satisfying `P_N J P_N = 0`.  This must be verified
from its form; one safe form is `J=(Nhat-N)X` with `[X,Nhat]=0` (or its
Hermitian symmetrization).  Let

```
H - b I = S + R + J,       P_N J P_N = 0,
S = sum_(a=1)^k q_a B_a^dagger B_a,   q_a >= 0.
```

The displayed terms are the retained SOS certificate.  The question is how to
bound `||P_N R P_N||` using a small coefficient description.

Partition the modes into `p` clusters of size at most `w`.  Assume that `R` is
given in a *local coefficient decomposition*

```
R = sum_(X in E) R_X,
supp(R_X) subset of X,   |X| <= r,
```

where `E` is a hypergraph of cluster supports, `d` is its maximum degree, and
each `R_X` is represented in a fixed, normalized local operator basis by at
most `s` rational coefficients.  The verifier is given rational upper bounds
`u_X >= ||R_X||` obtained locally (for example, exact diagonalization of the
at-most-`w r`-mode algebra, interval arithmetic, or a sum-of-squares identity).
No global matrix for the `binom(M,N)`-dimensional sector is part of the input.

## Theorem (local-overlap compression)

Under the setting above, the identity is a valid lower-bound certificate

```
E_0(H; N) >= b - eta,       eta = sum_(X in E) u_X,
```

and it has a verifier whose arithmetic work is

```
O(k * s_B^2 * normcost(w_B) + |E| * s * 2^(O(wr)) * bitcost(u_X))
```

and whose operator-description length is `O(k + |E| s)`, apart from the
bit-length of the rational coefficients.  If each cluster participates in at
most `d` supports, then `|E| <= p d`; hence for fixed `w,r,s,d` the certificate
description is linear in the number of clusters, regardless of the dimension
of the fixed-`N` Fock sector.

### Proof

For every normalized `N`-fermion vector `psi`, the explicitly checked `J` term
vanishes after projection and positivity
gives

```
<psi|H|psi> - b = sum_a q_a ||B_a psi||^2 + <psi|R|psi>.
```

The triangle inequality and compression to the sector give

```
<psi|R|psi> >= -||P_N R P_N||
                 >= -sum_X ||P_N R_X P_N||
                 >= -sum_X u_X = -eta.
```

Taking the infimum over normalized `psi` proves the energy bound.  Each local
coefficient block is verified in an algebra with at most `w r` modes; exact
local diagonalization or interval norm computation can cost `2^(O(wr))`, so
this is efficient only when `w r` is bounded.  The `s_B^2` factor accounts for
combining retained operator words.  No basis vector of the global sector is
constructed.  The bound `|E| <= p d` is double counting of cluster incidences.
This is an elementary local-norm argument, not a new fact about
N-representability.

## What this theorem does and does not buy

The theorem separates three claims that are often conflated:

1. **Existence:** a short SOS plus local residual decomposition exists.
2. **Verification:** once supplied, its lower bound is checked with local work.
3. **Discovery:** finding the `B_a`, `q_a`, supports, and `u_X` is efficient.

The theorem proves (2), and gives a size guarantee conditional on (1).  It does
not prove (1) for generic Coulomb Hamiltonians and says nothing by itself about
(3).  A priced dictionary can still contain exponentially many candidate
operators before a short certificate is found.  A certificate is therefore
not an RIP theorem and does not establish universal chemical scalability.

The fixed-`N` qualification matters.  A two-sided ideal generated by
`Nhat-N` cannot be used as a shortcut: the finite CAR algebra is matrix-simple,
so that ideal is generally the whole algebra, and arbitrary multipliers need
not vanish after projection.  A local operator norm on the unrestricted
Fock space is a safe upper bound, but it can be very loose.  A sharper bound may
use local particle-number sectors, provided the verifier proves that the
restriction is valid.  Likewise, locality here is locality in the selected
orbital/cluster basis; an orbital rotation can make the support hypergraph
dense and destroy the linear-size conclusion.  The condition is therefore
basis dependent by design.

The additive residual `eta` is the meaningful error quantity.  A statement such
as “the certificate captures `1-epsilon` of the optimum” is not invariant under
energy shifts or rescaling and is not a usable accuracy guarantee.

## A concrete discovery hypothesis

For a localized active space, build a support hypergraph from the one- and
two-body coefficient tensor after orbital localization.  Price only dressed
operators `B_a` whose CAR-normal form has support in one hyperedge or in a
bounded-radius neighborhood.  At each round, retain a candidate only if:

* its exact SOS contribution is positive on the fixed-`N` sector;
* its omitted terms admit local rational bounds `u_X`; and
* the exact lower bound improves by more than the newly added residual budget.

The empirical prediction is that weakly entangled, geometrically local active
spaces will show bounded `w,r,s,d` and a certificate gap that decreases with
the number of priced neighborhoods.  The falsifier is a family where local
coefficients remain bounded but every valid lower-bound certificate needs
nonlocal support or `eta` stays extensive.  This test targets the structural
condition directly and avoids treating a faster LP restart as evidence of
physical compressibility.

## Adversarial boundary

No bounded-support theorem can cover arbitrary fixed-`N` states.  The states
`(|123> + |456>)/sqrt(2)` and `(|123> - |456>)/sqrt(2)` have identical one- and
two-body marginals but opposite expectation of the three-body coherence
`a_1^dagger a_2^dagger a_3^dagger a_6 a_5 a_4 + h.c.`.  Thus a two-body
certificate cannot be promoted into a universal description of all observables
or thermal entropy.  The theorem is only an energy lower-bound statement for a
supplied two-body Hamiltonian and a supplied local residual decomposition.

## Status

This is a candidate sufficient theorem, not a scalability result for molecular
Coulomb systems.  The elementary ingredients are positivity, the operator-norm
triangle inequality, fixed-sector ideal reduction, and local finite-dimensional
verification.  The research problem is to prove or measure the hypothesis for a
nontrivial chemical family, while separately benchmarking dictionary discovery.
