# Broad search map for certified molecular energy bounds

Prepared 13 September 2026 UTC. This is a research portfolio, not an exhaustive taxonomy of all future mathematics. A route can be worth investigating without a known efficient algorithm. None of the speculative combinations below is claimed novel without a prior-art search, or claimed to solve generic chemistry.

The objective is a certified interval for the ground energy of a specified finite Hamiltonian, initially width at most 0.0016 Ha, with discovery, upper-state construction, verification, memory, and precision all counted. This is different from a guarantee on basis-set error or on experimentally measured chemistry.

The latest local [scoreboard](../../results/certificate_scaling/active_space_ladder_latest.json) already records H4–H10 passing that width target. H10's recorded width is 0.001157618253846804 Ha with 4,674,900 discovery Gram entries and an FCI-derived upper witness. These are existing receipts inspected during this investigation, not new replays. The unresolved issue is compact, affordable discovery and transfer beyond this finite ladder.

## What to keep and what to correct in the supplied map

Keep broad hypothesis generation, independent checking, a critic, cost accounting, failed attempts, and a ladder from tractable controls to molecular fixtures. Expand the allowed representations and proof languages. Forcing every new idea to fit the current sparse CAR JSON schema would exclude some of the most interesting routes before testing them.

1. **Chordal sparsity is conditional.** Localized Coulomb interactions generally retain long-range terms; small coefficients are not exact zeros. Planarity does not imply bounded treewidth: square grids are a counterexample. A chordal PSD-completion theorem is not a theorem that a dense fermionic SOS relaxation can be replaced by local cliques at the same accuracy. CAR products, global constraints, coefficient precision, and solver iterations also enter cost. The stated universal O(M 4^k) solve cost is unsupported. [Sparse noncommutative optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00569) provides genuine sparsity hierarchies, with explicit assumptions.

2. **Telescoping preserves an identity, not an accuracy guarantee.** When two cluster terms use exactly the same embedded boundary operator with opposite signs, their sum cancels. This can improve a decomposed lower bound by redistributing boundary energy. It does not prove that the sum of cluster minima is close to the global minimum. The repository's [positive-chain experiment](../side_routes_20260913/README.md) already gives an extensive-width counterexample for a particular local witness. That excludes that witness, not every larger cluster construction.

3. **Spin restrictions need an ablation, not an assumed cure.** An SU(2)-invariant operator is not necessarily supported only in the physical singlet sector. For an invariant Hamiltonian, a closed dictionary, and an invariant exact certificate cone, group averaging permits invariant witnesses without losing the exact identity. The coordinate coefficient-L1 penalty need not be invariant, so restricted multipliers can affect numerical repair and the residual objective. This is documented in [spin_irrep_frontier.md](spin_irrep_frontier.md). The inspected material does not prove that unrestricted unphysical-sector X fixes the current convergence problem. Existing checking still requires X to be Hermitian and number conserving.

4. **TTN is a useful ansatz, not a small-bond theorem.** A tree cannot match every edge of a cyclic molecular graph. Removing a poor linear ordering does not remove physical entanglement across tree cuts. Given a state, certified contraction can provide a variational upper endpoint; constructing a sufficiently accurate low-bond state remains a separate task. [Nakatani and Chan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2298) establish the chemistry method. General PEPS contraction has serious worst-case complexity barriers: [Schuch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611050).

5. **A 1-RDM does not eliminate the difficult functional.** The ensemble occupation constraints are simple for spin orbitals, but evaluating or tightly lower-bounding the interaction functional contains the many-body difficulty. Taking a Legendre–Fenchel envelope does not supply a cheap evaluation oracle. [Schuch and Verstraete](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.0483) make the related density-functional complexity obstruction explicit. This is a worst-case boundary, not a reason to abandon restricted molecular families.

6. **Optimal transport still needs quantum and numerical bounds.** The strictly correlated electron limit yields an optimal-transport problem; a discretized multi-marginal problem can itself have exponential size. Sinkhorn solves an entropically regularized problem, so a bound for the intended unregularized objective needs the appropriate dual-feasibility or regularization correction. Writing an exact Bohm quantum potential does not make its dependence on the unknown many-body state cheap. A relevant primary construction is [Khoo, Lin, Lindsey and Ying's SCE relaxation](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/20M1310977).

7. **Recovery is not arbitrary marginal representability.** A recovery channel can construct a positive state, but it need not reproduce all supplied overlapping marginals. Small conditional mutual information is a condition about a genuine state; it cannot simply be assumed for relaxed pseudo-marginals. Generic approximate-recovery guarantees use suitable recovery maps, not an unconditional guarantee for the displayed ordinary Petz expression. Ground-state closeness, fermionic parity/number, normalization, and accumulated energy error remain obligations. [Sutter, Fawzi and Renner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07251).

8. **Certificate continuation needs feasible certificates along the path.** A derivative of an optimized value under regularity conditions does not automatically specify an ODE for an entire feasible SOS factorization. Cone rank changes, conditioning, phase transitions, and spectral crossings require handling. Independent checking at each step remains useful even where smooth transport fails. Rigorous perturbative methods already exist under stated assumptions, for example [Bravyi, DiVincenzo and Loss](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0675).

9. **The verifier is a proof checker with a particular scope.** Its CLI is `python3 -S -m experiments.marginal_symbolic --verify certificate.json`, run from the repository root. The positional `verify` command in the supplied prompt is incorrect. `verify` returns a lower bound; it does not by itself verify a trial state and return an upper bound. Existing spectral residual extensions also go beyond coefficient L1. Exact rational arithmetic and `-S` reduce some risks; they do not establish that the checker implements the correct theorem. Passing this format is sufficient evidence within its audited rules, not necessary for every possible mathematical certificate.

## Five combinations I would explicitly investigate

These are research proposals, not novelty claims. The point is to specify a mechanism and a decisive first test.

**A. Trial state plus a certified complement.** Let normalized u have theta=<u,Hu>, Q=I-|u><u|, and r=QHu. If QHQ >= mu Q on the complement with mu>theta, then

\[
\theta-\frac{\|r\|^2}{\mu-\theta}\le E_0(H)\le\theta.
\]

The inequality is a standard Schur-complement consequence, derived in the background agent's [mathematical attempt](brainstorm_theory_attempt_20260913.md). Search for a cheap parent-Hamiltonian or local SOS proof of mu. A small residual alone cannot supply mu: an excited eigenvector also has zero residual. First compare parent/SOS complement costs against the full lower-bound search on a small chain. The new research target is a cheap construction, not the displayed inequality.

**B. Bound the error relative to a positive reference.** Suppose H=E_ref I+P+R, P>=0, and an explicitly certified normalized u satisfies Pu=0. If

\[
R\ge-\eta P-\varepsilon I,\qquad 0\le\eta\le1,
\]

then H>= (E_ref-epsilon)I. If additionally <u,Ru><=delta, the interval width is at most epsilon+delta. The proof is simply H >= E_ref I+(1-eta)P-epsilon I and the variational upper bound. This can remain useful when the global norm of R is extensive: the positive reference absorbs part of R. The hard, unproved chemistry step is discovering compact P and the relative inequality. Test on a perturbed interacting parent family and compare against the existing coefficient/spectral residual bounds.

**C. Local correlation blocks plus global Coulomb channels.** Retain long-range Coulomb structure through a small set of collective density factors, then use local fermionic SOS/Schur blocks only for the remaining correlation. This could avoid paying a norm penalty for simply deleting the Coulomb tail. First measure the actual channel rank, retained Gram/map size, and certified error on the unchanged H4/H6 fixtures. Abandon the claimed benefit if factorization is compact but the mixed Gram map becomes dense. Prior density fitting and low-rank work exists; the candidate is their integration into a complete certificate.

**D. Adapt buffer sizes to a proved total-error budget.** Try to establish an error law sum_i C_i exp(-r_i/xi_i), then choose each buffer r_i according to its contribution and verification cost. For uniform constants, r=O(log(M/epsilon)) controls a total error of order M exp(-r/xi). In 1D a cost exponential in r can then be polynomial in M/epsilon; in 3D a ball may contain order r^3 sites, so that inference does not automatically hold. The required error law is a hypothesis and is not implied by correlation decay alone. The repo already identified the uniform-range version; the proposal is certified adaptive allocation and a geometry-aware cost analysis.

**E. Cover a parameter path with independently verified certificates.** Keep several parent Hamiltonians or trial subspaces and switch between them as geometry/coupling changes. Each local region supplies a valid endpoint and a perturbation enclosure. This avoids requiring a single smooth Gram factorization across rank changes. A plain safe transfer is L(H') >= L(H)-||H'-H||, but this may be far too loose; the research question is whether relative/blockwise perturbation makes a useful finite cover. Test a stretched-bond path including an avoided crossing and report required patch count. A reaction-energy variant needs an explicit joint error theorem, not presumed cancellation.

## A less restrictive agent prompt

> Find a cheaper way to produce a rigorously certified ground-energy interval for the Spectra molecular fixtures. You may change the operator representation, trial-state family, decomposition, or proof strategy, and may propose a new mathematical construction. Read the current results first: H10 already passes 0.0016 Ha with expensive discovery and an FCI-derived upper witness. Generate several materially different ideas, select one, and attempt the smallest decisive lemma, counterexample, or experiment. State assumptions and a stopping rule before running. Use exact or rigorously bounded arithmetic for claimed endpoints; treat numerical solvers as proposal tools. Count discovery, candidate pricing, state construction, memory, precision, and verification. Preserve failures and distinguish existing mathematics from an unproved idea. Return a valid improvement, a proved obstruction for the attempted family, or a precise unresolved lemma. Start with trial-state/complement certificates, or explain a more promising choice.

For the existing CAR format, replay the lower endpoint with:

```sh
python3 -S -m experiments.marginal_symbolic --verify certificate.json
```

A separate appropriate upper-state checker is required. A new proof format needs its own sound, independently checked rules; it must not silently weaken the existing checks. Use small dense problems as references with costs disclosed. Use explicit compute budgets for large tests instead of making an arbitrary M>8 prohibition part of the mathematics.

## How I would allocate the research effort

Keep the verified baseline, and investigate three complementary questions: can the upper witness become cheap (MPS/TTN/selected CI), can the lower proof use global low-rank plus adaptive local structure, and can a new theorem connect them (Schur, relative bounds, or controlled continuation)? Choose the smallest decisive experiment for each. Share useful proved lemmas and counterexamples between attempts. Promote a route based on total certified width per full cost across held-out fixtures, not raw matrix dimensions or a solver status.

The generator/critic/checker architecture is a good foundation, but a proof assistant is only as relevant as the theorem formalized, its assumptions, and its connection to the intended physical model. A program-specific exact checker is valuable and different from a formal proof of that program's soundness.

On the Navier–Stokes comparison, [OpenAI's September 8 account](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) says discovery used an internal model more capable than GPT-6 Astra and about 10,000 coordinating agents; Astra did subsequent Lean formalization and verification. This session can run available agents and reuse the workflow. It does not have demonstrated access to that internal discovery model or the original agent population.
