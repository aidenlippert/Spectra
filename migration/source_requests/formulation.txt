2. The Algebraic Engine: Where "Short Certificates" Come From

How do we prove that a proposed inequality \langle A, \Gamma \rangle \ge b is
universally valid for all N-fermion states, without checking an exponential
Hilbert space?

We use the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR):
\{a_i, a_j^\dagger\} = \delta_{ij} I, \qquad \{a_i, a_j\} = 0

A certificate is short if the lifted operator can be written as a low-rank sum
of squares of fermionic operators:

\widehat{A} - bI = \sum_{\alpha=1}^m B_\alpha^\dagger B_\alpha + \sum_k \lambda_k \big(\hat{N} - N\big) + R

Where:

  - m is small (only a handful of test operators, e.g., m \le 10, rather than
    all \binom{M}{3} combinations).
  - Each B_\alpha is a targeted linear combination of 2-body or 3-body
    operators:
    B_\alpha = \sum c_{ijk}^{(\alpha)} \, a_i a_j a_k + \sum d_{ij}^{(\alpha)} \, a_i a_j
  - \hat{N} = \sum a_i^\dagger a_i enforces particle-number conservation.
  - R is a tiny numerical residual with certified norm \|R\| \le \eta.

Why this is a revolution:

Because B_\alpha^\dagger B_\alpha is positive semi-definite by construction on
every quantum state:
\langle \Psi | B_\alpha^\dagger B_\alpha | \Psi \rangle = \| B_\alpha | \Psi \rangle \|^2 \ge 0
Evaluating whether the certificate is valid does not require diagonalizing an
exponential Hamiltonian. It only requires verifying the algebraic commutation
expansion in Lean 4 / FLINT to confirm that the operator identity holds!
