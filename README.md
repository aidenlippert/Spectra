## Second investigation: complementary learning

The new result has an exact mathematical claim: in the admitted product-Pauli experiment family, one-shot errors are `7/16`, `3/8`, `3/8`, and `1/4` with neither, either, or both response laws. Their complementary gain is `1/16`. A first noisy acquisition compiles the experiment for the second, with exact binomial coverage and all primitive shots counted. An r-factor extension has successive marginal gains that double, subject to explicit source and noise assumptions.

Three hidden-mask systems recovered both laws, and exact evaluation covered 3,249 unused control pairs per system. Matched structured retrieval and conventional inference tie the learner. The proof establishes finite complementary transfer, not a superior general learning algorithm. A separate revision experiment discovers two omitted pair interactions and rejects a cubic mismatch; it reports **zero** warm measurement savings.

The source is an admitted, classically simulable physical model; no hardware experiment was performed. At that earlier stage: **106 passing tests**, including exact certificate corruption checks, wrong-source assumptions, insufficient-data refusal and model mismatch. [The full proof](research/v2/PROOF.md), [source context](research/v2/lifelong_learning_sources.md), and [statistical audit](research/v2/statistical_audit.md) make the limits reviewable.

## Fourth investigation: a learned-policy null and an exact obstruction

A policy learner trained in two stages did not gain from its second update on 32 held-out coupled quantum design contexts. The exact signed-moment planner was more accurate and cheaper in the measured implementation. [The V4 report](results/v4/REPORT.md) retains all comparisons, acquisition costs and risk certificates.

An exact counterexample shows that all twelve per-action features can agree while the optimal first actions are disjoint. A [higher-order theorem](research/v4/higher_order_obstruction.md) proves why growing experimental horizons can require higher interaction information. This guides the next attack; it does not establish a learned physical representation. The broad goal remains active.

The follow-up [calibration bridge](research/v4/calibration_theorem.md) acquires the necessary joint moments from sampled labelled outcomes, with no likelihood-table access by the learner. Four executions recover optimal policies with explicit confidence and acquisition counts. The moment vocabulary and source assumptions remain supplied; the bridge is not a second compounding result.

## Fifth investigation: acquired representations with complementary value

V5 acquires unknown dense mode directions through noisy binary readings of a declared coupled Gaussian source. Each acquired direction helps select a later physical probe. A common-target theorem proves that the two acquired bits together exceed their separate prediction benefits by at least **0.0779122 in error probability**, after conservative uncertainty and acquisition corrections.

The construction also gives two successive positive read savings after charging acquisition. In 64 simulated transfer trees, cumulative modes use **21 reads** per tree, frozen-after-first uses **37**, and scratch uses **56** at the same observed error. Structured replay and stateful Bayes tie at 21. A separate controlled-retention study covers 2,048 fresh targets; all source records and exact mismatch certificates are retained.

[The V5 report](results/v5/REPORT.md) includes 106 passing tests, source-record replay, computational profiling and full primitive acquisition counts. The [proof](research/v5/PROOF.md) states the supplied grammar, extremely demanding physical scales, assumed reset primitive and lack of hardware validation. This establishes bounded operational compounding, not open-ended discovery or algorithmic superiority. [The requirement audit](research/v5/goal_audit.md) records the distinction.
